The whole day, from the first job to the proof it was done

One object moves from booked to dispatched to on site to complete to paid. The office sees it, the technician sees it, the customer sees a window, and the proof is attached before anyone leaves.

What the field operation gets

The point is that the office and the truck are looking at the same thing at the same time.

  • Jobs with a real record

    Customer, service, technician, time window, price, duration, notes, photos and signature on one object.

  • A dispatch map

    Today's work and every technician's last known position on one map, so a late job is visible before it becomes a call.

  • Day routes that make sense

    Sequence a technician's stops, with the total distance stated and the option to re-optimise before the day starts.

  • Proof of work

    Photo, signature, timestamp and location captured at the job, attached to the record and available to the customer.

  • Technicians as real people

    Skills, home base, schedule and availability, so work is assigned to someone who can actually do it.

  • Check in and out

    A technician checks in when they arrive and out when they leave, which is what makes the mileage and the time on site real numbers.

The job lifecycle, and what is enforced at each step

Status is not a label someone types. It is a state the platform checks, and a transition that is not allowed is refused with the ones that are.

  • Booked
    The job is created with a customer, a service and a time. The address is placed on the map so the job can be routed, and the booking is checked against real capacity before it is confirmed.
  • Assigned
    A technician is chosen. Skills and service area are respected, so work does not land on someone who cannot do it or who is hours away.
  • Dispatched
    The technician has the job and the customer has a window. If the day moves, the affected customers are notified rather than left waiting.
  • On site
    Checked in, with the arrival time recorded from the technician's device. This is where the time on site and the mileage start being real numbers rather than estimates.
  • Complete
    The work is done and the proof is attached. If the job type requires a photo or a signature, the job cannot be closed without one, so the file is complete at the moment of closure rather than a week later.
  • Billed
    The completed job carries its parts, its labour and its notes into the invoice. Nothing is re-keyed from a paper form, and the price was agreed when the job was booked.

    Today's jobs, in order, with the address and the customer's notes. One button to check in, one to check out, one to attach a photo, one to take a signature. The next job is one tap away and the driving directions open in the phone's own maps.

    Why the routing numbers say straight line

    Route sequencing measures straight line distance between stops, and the interface says so. That is not a limitation to be embarrassed about, it is a decision about honesty: a dispatcher who catches a system claiming road accuracy it does not have stops trusting every other number it produces.

    Straight line ordering gets a day of eight to fifteen stops very close to the right sequence, which is the part that saves the fuel. Where road distance genuinely changes the answer, the platform supports a road based routing engine as a configuration rather than pretending.

    The same rule applies to arrival estimates. An estimate is labelled as an estimate, and the platform would rather show a range it can stand behind than a precise time it cannot.

    A job is not finished when the work is done. It is finished when the record, the proof and the invoice agree.

    Questions people ask before signing up

    Can I assign the same technician to two jobs at once?

    The platform will let you if you force it, but it will tell you first. Booking is checked against the capacity rules you set at the office, the area and the individual level, and an override is recorded so it is visible later rather than invisible forever.

    Does the technician need a company phone?

    No. The field view is a web page that installs to the home screen of whatever phone the technician carries, including a personal one. Nothing needs to be installed from an app store.

    How does the platform handle a job that runs long?

    The remaining stops for that day can be re-sequenced, and the affected customers can be notified with a new window. Because the customer's window comes from the same record the dispatcher is moving, there is no second list to keep in step.

    Is there a paper option?

    A completed job can be printed or exported with its photos, signature and notes, which is what most companies need when a customer or an insurer asks for documentation.

    Can customers see where the technician is?

    Yes, on the plans that include tracking. The customer receives a link that shows their own job and nothing else, and the link expires when the job is done.
